Vision

Any mixed waste in. Clean raw materials out.

We are building toward one factory that takes any mixed waste composite and turns it into clean, raw material streams that a manufacturer can buy and put straight into a new product. No factory does this today. Everything we build now is a step toward it.

What we optimize for

$ / ton

Cost to turn one ton of mixed waste into clean raw materials. Lower is better.

One number decides whether the factory is real. Every model, machine, and project we take on has to move it down. If it does not, it is a distraction, however good it looks in a pilot.

  • It is a price, not a promise. When the cost per ton lands below what virgin material costs, recycling stops depending on subsidies and regulation to survive.
  • It covers the whole chain. Handling, sorting, cleaning, and separation are all inside the number. A step that improves purity but doubles cost is not progress.
  • It hides nothing. Residue we cannot sell still shows up as cost per ton, so we cannot make the number look good by ignoring the hard part of the input.

There is no baseline for it yet

Today the number only exists in pieces: gate fees per ton, sorting cost per ton at a recovery facility, and the market price of each recovered fraction. For a genuinely mixed composite input and a clean raw material output, there is nothing to benchmark against, because nobody runs that process end to end. Part of the work is making the number measurable before we can make it small.

The goal stack

Three levels, bottom one first.

The goal at the top is out of reach until the level below it works in a real facility, on real waste, at a cost someone will pay. So we build upward.

  1. Goal Not possible today

    A factory for any mixed waste composite

    Multi-material packaging, textile blends, insulation, mixed construction waste. It goes in as it arrives and leaves as clean raw material streams, specified well enough that a producer can use them without a compromise on quality.

  2. Subgoal Open problem

    Complex mixed waste into clean fractions

    Before material can be raw again it has to be separated. That means a system that takes genuinely mixed, contaminated, unpredictable input and produces fractions clean enough to be sold as one material rather than as a mix.

  3. Subgoal What we build now

    Automated recycling stations

    Recycling stations and waste plants are where mixed waste first arrives and where the sorting decisions are made today, mostly by people. Automating detection, guidance, and control here gives us the data, the hardware experience, and the revenue to build the levels above it.

Why none of this exists yet

  • Composites are bonded. A drink carton, a coated textile, a turbine blade: several materials fused into one object. Mechanical recycling returns a downgraded mix, so most of it is burned or landfilled.
  • Purity thresholds are strict. Buyers of raw material need a narrow specification. Mixed input rarely reaches it without manual sorting.
  • The economics do not close. Where a technical route exists, cost per ton usually sits far above virgin material, so it never reaches scale.

What we measure in the meantime

  • Cost per ton for every automated step we put into operation.
  • Purity per output fraction, measured on real material rather than estimated.
  • Share of incoming material that leaves as a sellable fraction instead of residue.
  • Human hours per ton still needed to reach that purity.

These are proxies. They are useful because they move in the same direction as the number we actually care about.

We would rather be measured on the number than on the mission.
